# Interview record A

**Shared kitchen, social cooking and bulk shopping**

The participant works in media, shares a Sydney house with four other people, and regularly hosts meals. The responses below are anonymised English paraphrases of the 15 interview prompts.

| Topic | Response summary |
| --- | --- |
| Household and eating habits | Enjoys social meals such as brunch, barbecues, hotpot and taco nights. Often invites friends to eat together. |
| Cooking frequency | Cooks for themself two to three times a week, hosts once or twice, and uses leftovers afterwards. |
| Meal decisions | Uses social media for inspiration. Plans a theme for gatherings and improvises from the fridge on ordinary days. |
| Weekly pattern | Shops in bulk on Friday, preps on Saturday and works through the extra food over the next few days. |
| Shopping | Makes a list for events, but impulse purchases can displace everyday essentials. |
| Inventory | Uses gaps in a shared fridge. Clear boxes briefly help, but ingredients get mixed when the kitchen gets busy. Unrecognised items often become communal. |
| Date checks | Clears the fridge about every two to three weeks, generally when they need space for the next event. |
| Forgotten ingredients | Extra limes or herbs bought for a specific use get buried and spoil. |
| Local shopping | Bulk prices are appealing but space is scarce. Specialty ingredients can disappear in a crowded kitchen. |
| Effect of sharing | Clutter limits prep space. They report uneven cooling in a very full fridge. |
| Waste and budget | Feels guilty discarding food after events and sees waste as money they could spend elsewhere. |
| Stressful moments | Discovering an empty packet or missing ingredient after guests arrive is stressful. |
| Existing tools | Uses Splitwise for expenses. A labelling whiteboard did not stick, and stock is checked visually. |
| Desired help | Would value quantity planning for an event, missing-item checks and a suggestion that uses vegetables before their date. |
| Desired change | Wants clearer ownership and reminders for running low on frequently used event supplies. |
